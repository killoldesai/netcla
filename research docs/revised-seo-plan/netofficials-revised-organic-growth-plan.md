# Netofficials: revised organic lead generation plan

Prepared 4 October 2026. Planning horizon: 180 days from implementation start. This document replaces the original blanket expansion approach with a focused plan for custom software development, website design and development, SEO, SEM and PPC. India-based delivery and global customers are the working positioning. No website changes have been made.

## 1. Strategic thesis: why this buyer should choose Netofficials

**Netofficials can compete by connecting software and website delivery with search marketing under one accountable team.** A client who builds a product or website with us can continue into SEO and paid-search work without finding another supplier or repeating the handover. The opportunity in this competitive set sits between the software-development firms and the search-marketing agencies: own the buyer's journey from building the digital asset to acquiring customers through it. Win through continuity of requirements, implementation and measurement, supported by clear service scope and real delivery evidence. This is our chosen competitive position; the exports do not establish that we are the only firm capable of delivering it.

### Competitive frame

The five IT benchmarks represent software-delivery competitors; RankingBySEO and PageTraffic represent search-marketing competitors. The original benchmark presents limited marketing coverage among the IT firms, while the supplied US exports demonstrate substantial SEO competition from the agencies. The strategic gap is **ownership of the combined build-and-growth proposition within the software-development category**, rather than an absence of competition for SEO keywords. The exports do not establish competitors' full capabilities or prove that agencies cannot deliver custom software. Netofficials should make the combined proposition explicit and demonstrate how it benefits the buyer.

Put that distinction into the offer: a shared discovery brief, a named point of accountability, websites built with search and conversion requirements considered early, and a documented transition into ongoing marketing. Establish the actual delivery process before promising these features. Separate service enquiries remain available for clients who need only one part.

### Strategic decisions

Build two connected commercial branches under one brand:

1. **Software and websites:** custom software, business websites, website design, redesign, and selected ecommerce/MVP/SaaS work where delivery evidence exists.
2. **Search marketing:** SEO and paid search, with specialist services and pricing pages added when their scope is established.

Suggested positioning for review: **“We build business software and websites, then help customers acquire leads through SEO and paid search.”** Avoid implying every client requires all services. Give buyers separate enquiry routes for software, websites, SEO and PPC.

The primary objective is qualified organic enquiries, followed by sales opportunities and revenue. Organic visits to a PPC service page count toward this objective even though the service being sold is paid advertising. SEM/PPC are service offerings here; this plan does not require buying traffic.

Use existing URLs where their purpose is sound. Put content development, credible project evidence, search intent and conversion measurement ahead of creating a larger sitemap.

### Monday morning: five actions this week

1. SEO owner/developer: crawl the live site; record status codes, canonicals, indexability and internal links.
2. SEO owner: export the Search Console Page Indexing report and inspect representative priority URLs.
3. SEO owner/developer: reconcile all 57 GSC URLs with the crawl; mark live status, intended indexability and required corrections.
4. Developer/analytics owner: implement and test successful-enquiry tracking and CRM delivery; keep test events out of lead counts.
5. Business owner/SEO owner: agree qualified-lead criteria and organic attribution; finalize the tracked goal definition accordingly.

Record owners, outputs and unresolved defects by week's end. Complete these five actions before executing the page rollout or treating its baseline as implementation-ready.

## 4. Architecture and keyword ownership

Navigation should make **Software & Websites**, **SEO & PPC**, **Our Work**, **Insights**, **About** and **Contact** easy to find. `/services` acts as the directory; existing flat service URLs can remain. Folder depth is not itself a reason to migrate URLs.

The page plan has **47 mapped candidates**: 23 foundation/commercial/proof/conditional pages and 24 buyer guides. This is a sequenced backlog, not an instruction to publish 47 pages immediately. Nineteen candidates have at least one exact target phrase observed in a supplied competitor Organic export. Other candidates are explicitly labelled editorial research candidates, with no fabricated metrics.

The first-release target is five foundation improvements plus four main commercial pages: `/`, `/services`, `/about`, `/contact`, `/portfolio`, `/custom-software-development`, `/web-development`, `/seo-services`, and `/ppc-services`. Eight can describe the user-confirmed business without waiting for published case studies; `/portfolio` requires real publishable work. Launch eight if that evidence is unavailable and add the ninth when ready.

Keyword ownership rules:

- Homepage: brand, combined proposition and routing. The SEO service page owns nonbrand SEO service terms; the PPC page owns paid search service terms.
- Custom software: company/services/custom application phrases belong together initially. Industry and technology pages require a different scope and evidence.
- Web development: implementation, integrations and CMS engineering. Web design: UX, visual design and conversion structure. If the same portfolio and copy cannot distinguish them, combine the offering on `/web-development` rather than force separate pages.
- Website redesign: existing-site diagnosis, redesign and migration. It should add a different buying scenario from new-site development.
- SEO services: overall engagement. Packages: pricing and scope. Technical SEO: implementation and diagnosis. Local SEO: local-business needs. Publish subservices only when each has a distinct deliverable and credible evidence.
- SEM/PPC/Google Ads: one service page initially. Explain terminology and included channels. Split Google Ads or a broader SEM strategy page only if later target-country SERPs, service scope and query data support distinct intent.
- SEO vs PPC: one comparison article, linked from both services. Do not publish separate reversed-wording comparisons.
- Industry SEO/PPC pages: gated on sector fit. Do not mass-produce pages for accountants, dentists, doctors or cities simply because competitors rank for them.

Potential existing overlaps to inspect include `/qa-testing-services` versus `/software-testing-services`, `/privacy` versus `/privacy-policy`, and homepage variants `/home`, `/index.html`, `/index.php`. These are observations requiring live checks, not confirmed duplicate-content defects.

Use permanent redirects only when replacing a page with a materially equivalent destination. Update canonicals, internal links and sitemap entries together. Do not redirect unrelated discontinued services to the homepage. [Google URL migration guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)

## 5. Page briefs and publishing requirements

The CSV supplies an individual title, target phrases, scope, action, priority, phase, GSC baseline where available, and evidence gate for every mapped candidate. These standard briefs specify how to turn that map into publishable content.

### Publish now versus wait for proof

| Can publish after this week's technical checks | Hold until the specific evidence exists |
|---|---|
| Homepage, About, Contact and services directory describing the verified business and named team | Portfolio and case studies requiring real work, permission and substantiated outcomes |
| Custom software, website development, SEO and PPC pages describing the user-confirmed offerings, actual scope and process | Industry, ecommerce, MVP, SaaS and technology claims requiring relevant delivery evidence |
| Buyer guides reviewed by a competent subject-matter expert, with sources and clearly labelled examples | Awards, certifications, testimonials and performance/compliance claims requiring verification |

Published case studies are not a prerequisite for explaining an actual core service. Remove unsupported outcome claims and describe what the team can deliver accurately. A project example or testimonial remains evidence-gated wherever it appears, including on an otherwise publishable page. Pricing pages require approved commercial terms. **Launch a smaller site until the evidence supports expansion.** Do not fill a proof gap with generic or invented client stories. Existing specialist pages require review; this publishing split does not authorize automatic deletion.

### Custom software development

Buyer: a business needing a custom workflow, integration or application. Desired action: discuss requirements or request a scoped estimate.

Cover problems solved; supported project types; discovery and requirements; delivery milestones; testing and acceptance; integration approach; security responsibilities; maintenance; factors affecting cost; common buying questions. Add a real project with the team's precise contribution when publishable evidence is available; its absence need not block an accurate core-service page. Link to cost, software-versus-off-the-shelf and requirements guides. Avoid unverified enterprise scale, certifications, guaranteed savings or compliance claims.

### Website development, design and redesign

Buyer: a company needing a new business website or an improved existing site. Desired action: request a website scope or redesign review.

Specify content responsibilities, CMS choices supported by the team, integrations, responsive behavior, performance and accessibility work, migration steps, testing, launch and handover. Use portfolio examples and actual scope differences. Redesign content should explain URL/content inventory, redirect mapping, staging checks and post-launch search monitoring. Design content should show wireframes or design decisions, not generic claims about attractive websites. Link to website cost and redesign guides.

### SEO

Buyer: a business seeking qualified search demand and a clear delivery process. Desired action: request an initial assessment.

Explain baseline analysis, technical priorities, content and keyword ownership, internal linking, authority development, reporting and lead measurement. Present real results with dates, starting conditions, actions, outcomes and limitations. Packages must state inclusions, exclusions, commercial terms and who each engagement suits. Keep pricing enquiry available if public prices are not approved. Avoid guaranteed rankings and numerical forecasts unsupported by data.

### PPC/SEM

Buyer: a business needing accountable paid-search management. Desired action: request an account review or campaign scope.

Explain included platforms, campaign setup, conversion definitions, search-term exclusions, landing-page coordination, experiments, reporting and account access. Separate management fees from advertising spend. Case studies need dates and context for spend, lead volume, cost per lead and lead qualification. Do not imply Meta Ads are included unless confirmed. Link to pricing, PPC audit and landing-page guides.

### Case studies

Start with two or three usable projects, not a mandatory ten. Each study needs client context (or honest anonymization), problem, baseline, scope, Netofficials' role, approach, evidence of outcomes, timeline and relevant CTA. Obtain permission for names, logos, screenshots and testimonials. If commercial outcomes are unavailable, report substantiated implementation outcomes instead. No invented percentage improvements.

### Buyer guides

Answer one purchasing question. Include original examples, assumptions, tradeoffs, an expert reviewer, and a relevant service link. Cost articles must explain scope and estimate assumptions; do not invent standard market prices. Comparative articles should identify who each option suits. Publish evergreen material first and add a year only when the substance requires dated updates.

Google's guidance supports a clear purpose, firsthand expertise and useful original content. It does not impose a universal publication frequency or word count. [People-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

2. `/services` links to every active commercial page with a short description of its distinct scope.
3. Each service links to its relevant project proof and two or three useful buying guides as those become available.

5. Each case study links back to the service delivered and to a relevant enquiry route.
6. SEO/PPC pages can link to web design/redesign where landing-page or site issues matter. Software pages should not carry unrelated marketing links purely to increase link counts.

## 7. Technical and conversion work before scaling

Run a live crawl and manually inspect the nine first-release pages. Record response codes, indexability, canonicals, titles, headings, content, internal links and sitemap inclusion. Use Search Console URL Inspection for representative pages and review the Page Indexing report. A performance ZIP does not provide indexing diagnostics.

Check HTTPS and host normalization; trailing slash and homepage variants; robots rules; accidental noindex; JavaScript rendering of core content; mobile layout; form behavior; page speed; image weight; broken links; and duplicate templates. Confirm genuine problems before creating remediation tickets. Prioritize issues affecting active services.

Create a canonical URL inventory from crawl/CMS/sitemap and reconcile it with the 57 performance URLs and SEMrush landing pages. Check for existing marketing pages before adding the proposed slugs. Preserve useful pages until business-fit, content, query and backlink review supports a change. Low impressions or zero clicks alone are not deletion criteria.

Conversion implementation:

- Define a lead as a successfully submitted enquiry or confirmed consultation. Track CTA clicks separately as microconversions.
- Track form success only after successful server response, deduplicating repeated submissions. Do not count button clicks as leads.
- Route enquiries by software, website, SEO or PPC. Capture country, objective, timing and an optional budget range without making the form unnecessarily long.
- Record landing page, acquisition channel and permitted attribution fields in the CRM. Keep personal information out of analytics event payloads.
- Mark qualified leads with service fit, budget fit, timeline and contact validity. Record sales opportunity and closed revenue separately.
- Test desktop and mobile submissions, tracking and CRM delivery before declaring the conversion path ready.

Do not diagnose poor form conversion from eight search clicks. Establish reliable instrumentation now and evaluate conversion rates once the denominator is meaningful.

## 8. Delivery sequence

The schedule is a capacity assumption, not a guaranteed ranking timeline. Allow approximately four service/foundation updates and four expert-reviewed articles per month after initial setup; reduce breadth if proof collection or development takes longer. A marketer/SEO owner, subject-matter reviewers and a developer are assumed, but no staffing allocation has been supplied.

**Minimum content floor: two well-sourced, expert-reviewed buyer pieces per month once the content phase starts.** This is an operating commitment for this plan, not a Google ranking threshold. Below that pace, development of the planned clusters becomes too slow to meet the rollout and should be treated as stalled against this schedule. If capacity cannot sustain the floor, postpone the content phase explicitly, complete the core service work, assign a reviewer/writer and issue a revised start date. Do not publish weak pieces to meet a quota.

At four pieces per month, publish eight during days 31–90 and the remaining sixteen during days 91–180. At the minimum pace, publish four during days 31–90 and six during days 91–180; carry the remaining fourteen beyond day 180. Prioritize software cost, website cost, SEO cost and SEO vs PPC first. The CSV phase labels express topic priority; this capacity schedule governs actual delivery dates.

| Period | Deliverables | Completion gate |
|---|---|---|
| Days 0–14 | Baseline, live URL inventory, crawl, tracking design, proof collection, keyword report settings review | Core URLs and content ownership agreed; real defects documented |
| Days 15–30 | Four foundation improvements; four main commercial pages; portfolio only with proof; working tracked enquiry routes | Distinct service scope, accurate claims, indexable pages and tested forms |
| Days 31–60 | Web design/redesign if distinct; SEO packages; blog hub; case-study hub and two or three cases only when evidence is ready; first four buyer guides | Expert review and source/proof checks complete |
| Days 61–90 | Conditional ecommerce/MVP; technical SEO/local SEO if justified; PPC pricing; next four guides | Commercial readiness and target-market keyword/SERP validation |
| Days 91–180 | Remaining sixteen guides; selective SaaS/React/industry work; refresh based on data | Expansion justified by proof, qualified demand and delivery capacity |

The phase fields in the CSV are priority windows, subject to the capacity schedule above. A guide referencing a specialist service can launch only when that parent page is useful and available. Use the two-piece minimum schedule when the four-piece standard is not feasible, and move lower-priority dates accordingly.

The first 90 days should establish the commercial foundation and measurement. Do not promise first-page positions, a particular enquiry count or a fixed ROI from these exports.

## 9. Measurement and operating decisions

### What good looks like at day 90

| Checkpoint | Target and response if unmet |
|---|---|
| First-release pages | All nine target pages are live, canonical and confirmed indexed through GSC inspection; if portfolio proof is unavailable, all eight approved pages meet this target and the ninth is explicitly held. Investigate every unresolved indexing issue. |
| Enquiry measurement | Successful submissions are tested on desktop/mobile, deduplicated, delivered to the CRM and assigned a usable acquisition source; qualification and country fields are available. Repair tracking before judging demand. |
| Content delivery | Eight reviewed buyer pieces at standard capacity, or four under the explicitly adopted minimum schedule, linked to available services. Reschedule gaps with named owners. |
| Commercial signal | Working target: at least one real qualified organic enquiry recorded since tracking went live. A branded Google search counts if attributed to organic; a direct visit is recorded separately and does not count toward the organic target. |

The enquiry target is a management checkpoint, not a prediction supported by the eight-click baseline. If no qualified organic enquiry is recorded, report the missed target plainly and review relevant visibility, organic sessions, offer, proof and conversion behavior before expanding. Test submissions never count as enquiries. Indexing is also subject to Google's processing; unresolved exclusions require diagnosis rather than a declaration of success.

Primary scorecard, monthly by service and country:

| Metric | Definition | Baseline availability |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified organic leads | Valid organic enquiries meeting agreed commercial criteria | Not supplied; instrument |
| Organic sales opportunities | Qualified leads accepted into the sales pipeline | Not supplied; CRM |
| Organic closed revenue | Revenue from attributable organic opportunities | Not supplied; CRM |
| Successful organic enquiries | Form success/confirmed bookings from organic sessions | Not supplied; analytics |
| Organic enquiry rate | Successful enquiries divided by organic landing sessions | Not calculable from GSC clicks |
| Nonbrand commercial clicks | GSC clicks on an explicitly reviewed commercial-query set | Establish set; anonymized queries remain unavailable |
| Priority-page visibility | Clicks, impressions and position by mapped page/query | Supplied historical baseline; refresh monthly |
| Technical health | Indexability and crawl defects affecting active pages | Requires crawl and indexing reports |

Use comparable full periods and the same filters. The supplied monthly counts include partial June and September, so do not interpret month-to-month totals as an unqualified trend. Use rolling 28-day comparisons and country/device breakdowns during implementation, keeping any seasonality and small samples visible.

Every four weeks:

- Review commercial queries and pages gaining visibility. Prioritize proven service fit over total impressions.
- When a relevant query has sustained impressions around positions 8–30, inspect intent, proof, content gaps and internal links before editing.
- When a page ranks well for intended queries but receives few clicks, compare its actual SERP title/snippet with competing results. Evaluate query-level data rather than page averages alone.
- When organic visits arrive without qualified enquiries, inspect landing-page message, proof, CTA, offer and form functionality. Segment by service and country.
- When similar owned pages repeatedly appear for the same buyer task, review consolidation using page-level query exports and content comparison. Multiple ranking URLs alone do not prove harmful cannibalization.
- If new pages are missing or not indexed, investigate technical/indexing causes before accelerating output.
- Expand a vertical or specialist service only after expertise and customer fit are established. Do not use domain authority as the principal success condition.

At day 90, select the branch with the best combination of qualified leads, credible enquiry quality and relevant search progress for the next allocation of effort. With low volumes, use customer evidence and sales feedback alongside search data rather than overinterpreting percentages.

## 10. Country strategy and remaining validation

**Working country decision: serve global buyers from India-based English service pages, with commercial information and communication norms written for international clients.** State actual timezone overlap, meeting arrangements, milestones, handover/support expectations, and payment/currency terms; distinguish delivery location from the customer's location. Publish prices or ranges only after commercial approval and label the currency and scope clearly. Support India enquiries through the same pages. Proceed with this model now and revisit geographic allocation at the day-90 review using recorded country-level qualified leads and opportunities. If the sample is still too small, retain the model and review monthly. Additional country keyword data refines targeting without postponing this decision. Do not invent overseas offices.

The US is the initial global keyword research market, supported by confirmed US competitor exports and current impressions, but is not a confirmed highest-converting market. Obtain India exports separately and supplement US keyword coverage, then compare buyer intent and service fit. Add UK/Australia or other markets only with a business rationale. A country page needs distinctive information and demand; replacing country names in generic copy creates little buyer value and can fall within doorway-abuse patterns. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#doorway-abuse)

Remaining inputs improve precision but do not prevent using this plan:

1. SEMrush report-month/filter confirmation for all files; US database confirmed by the user.
2. India and selected global country Keyword Magic/Overview and keyword-gap exports, especially for website design, local/technical SEO, Google Ads and buyer-guide phrases absent from current exports.
3. Project examples, approved results, supported platforms, real sector experience and permission to publish.
4. Current crawl/CMS inventory, indexing reports, sitemap, backlinks and target-market SERP review.
5. Ideal customer size, minimum engagement budget, sales qualification criteria, team capacity and content budget.
6. Analytics/CRM enquiry history and a definition of an acceptable lead.

The absence of an exact phrase in these competitor exports does not mean zero demand. Conversely, a high-volume phrase does not establish lead value. Candidates without measured evidence remain explicit research tasks in the page map.
